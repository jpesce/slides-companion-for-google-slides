# A website for the extension

**Problem:** the only places to learn about the extension are its Chrome Web Store listing and the GitHub README, which is written for people who read code. A presenter who hears about it, or finds it in a search, has nowhere to see what it does at a glance, watch it, and add it. The store's "Homepage URL" and the repository's website point at the store listing itself.

**Evidence of demand:** the store listing and the README both ask for a homepage; every related project in BENCHMARK.md with a following has a page of its own. The maintainer asked for one on 15 November 2026, in "Google's language meets Apple's craft and minimalism", hosted for free on GitHub Pages, to replace the Wikipedia page in the links screenshot too (card 570).

**Approach:** one page, in `site/`, put together by `scripts/build-site.mjs` into `build/site/`, which GitHub Pages publishes from a workflow (`.github/workflows/site.yml`). What it shares with the extension and the brand is filled in as it's put together, so it can't fall behind: the companion's drawing (`scripts/build-brand.mjs`) and how he moves (`extension/companion-motion.js`, split out of `companion.js` so both use it), Settings' illustrations, the icon, and the languages in `_locales/`. Its pictures are the store screenshots' crops (`scripts/demo/screenshots.mjs`), drawn from `npm run demo`'s stills by `npm run site:pictures`, with a lighter copy of the demo video.

**Decisions (15 November 2026):**

- The story goes from the feeling to the tools, not the other way: the hero promises calm ("keeps an eye on the details, so you can keep yours on the room"), playing on the companion's eyes, and names no tool. The tools come as moments in a talk ("When the back row can't see"), each above what it does. "Free and open source" sits quietly next to each Add to Chrome, where people decide, linked to the code; the languages close the page, just before the footer. Then the little things, ending with "He never takes the stage": he fits into Slides as if he'd always been part of it, and tucks away; then how to start, and the last call. What keeps working (videos, animations, clickers) isn't listed: the page says what he adds, not what stays the same.
- The look is the store pictures': white, Google's lightest grey (#F1F3F4) for surfaces, ink for words and buttons, Slides yellow on the icon and once more, behind "superpowers". Google Sans Flex, headlines at 500, tracked in more as they grow (-0.022em at 72px). Material's 28px corners and pill buttons, an 8-point grid.
- Type grows with the window from a phone's 390px to a laptop's 1280px, then holds: headline 48 to 72px, section titles 32 to 48, card titles 24 to 32, words 17, buttons 48px tall. Of twenty pages measured at 1440 and 2560px wide, only Figma's and Loom's type grows past a laptop's width, and only a little. Pages for people rather than developers set it this big: Google's Chrome page 72 and 60 in the same family, Gamma 72 and 48, Apple 80 and 48 to 56; Linear's and Vercel's 64 suit a denser tool. A 64px scale was tried, against a report of a page too big that turned out to be the browser's zoom. The layout steps at Apple's breakpoints: one column on a phone, 692px on a tablet (734px up), and on a laptop 980px widening to 1200px by a 1440px window, as wide as Apple's, Linear's and Pitch's run there, then holding, so a 5K display gets a laptop's page with more room around it. From a tablet up, the countdown and the notes share a row, since stacked each was a screen tall.
- Nothing may be wider than the page: the languages' drifting rows once made it scroll sideways, so a trackpad swipe pushed the whole page off centre. A test checks the width at a phone's, a tablet's, a laptop's and a 5K display's.
- The little things go as the tools do: two cards side by side, then one across. Three in a row left the first two half empty beside the last one's longer words; on a wide screen the last lies on its side, its words left and its picture running off the card's foot. Features, in the bar, brings its title to just under the bar.
- Ready in a minute is words under three short rules, which fill in ink one after another as the page scrolls through them, like a progress bar, and empty as it scrolls back, so the reader sets the pace. The last step pays it off: "Present as usual. Only now, with a few superpowers.", the presenter changing nothing and getting more. Once the last rule is full, Slides yellow sweeps in behind "superpowers" like a highlighter, swells a little as a pen lifts, and a sheen crosses it, again every few seconds; sparkles burst around the word and keep twinkling for as long as it's highlighted, never in step, each at a new place around it, a golden ratio's step along its outline from the last, so they spread evenly (16 November 2026: above it and off its end only read as lopsided), mostly small, turning as they grow and fade. What's drawn follows the scroll through a short ease, so a mouse wheel's jumps glide. "A few" keeps the promise modest, as "a little help" does. Each number is a quiet label, not a badge, and on a phone the steps stack, each under its rule. Component libraries' steppers (ReUI, Mantine, Dice UI, Origin UI) are form wizards' dots and lines; the best pages' how-it-works either show the real screens, as Apple's Switch to iPhone page does, or set the steps as type. Three grey dots between two grey modules looked like a template, and a path of ink numbers ending on his icon put him three times on one screen.
- He's on the page where he means something, and never more than twice on one screen: he opens it (the icon, and himself peeking over the film), he's the subject once (his button in Slides' toolbar, in "He never takes the stage"), and he closes it, rising from the last call's edge. The bar keeps the icon beside the name, as the brand's mark; the footer gives the name in words. With his icon in the footer and in the steps too, he was four times on the last screen. On a tall window (a tablet upright, a 5K display) the bar's icon, his toolbar button and the last call's companion can share a screen for a moment as the steps pass.
- The film takes half of a laptop's first screen rather than a quarter (at 1440 by 830, a 13-inch MacBook's browser window): the hero is tighter there, and the companion's head rises beside the small print, off to the right, rather than below it. The film is as wide as everything else.
- The bar floats, as Raycast's does: a frosted white pill 16px below the window's top (12px on a phone), its corners a pill's like the buttons'. It reaches past the page's edges by its own 10px padding, so the icon starts where the page does and Add to Chrome ends where it ends, the button 10px inside it all round so the curves run parallel; the links sit in its middle, each a pill on hover. It isn't there at the top of the page, where the hero holds the same icon, name and button: it floats down into place once the hero's name has gone up under it, as the App Store's bar fills once the app's icon has, and stays rather than coming and going with the page's buttons. Over the picture tool's black tile, white glass turned a grey smudge, so it turns to dark glass there, Add to Chrome white, as Apple's bar does over a dark section. The film is light, so it stays white over it.
- One way to play the film, not two: the pill on its poster (with its length), or the poster itself. A "Watch the film" button beside Add to Chrome said the same thing a few lines above it, and left two buttons to choose between. On a phone, the wide tools' screens show their left part larger, bleeding off the right, since a whole screen shrunk to a phone's width can't be read.
- The hero plays the film on its own screen, sound on, when asked; its poster is the film's first picture slide, the pointer on the Earth, which the tile below then shows full screen. Play sits in the poster's top left corner, which every frame leaves clear, on a wide screen, and under the screen on a narrower one.
- The picture tool's tile is black, so the Earth floats in it; the others are grey, their screens bleeding off the right and bottom as on the store's screenshots. The countdown and the notes share a row: the notes window floats on its own, its top level with the countdown's screen. Its foot runs 24px past the tile's edge, so the tile crops it before its rounded corners (16 November 2026: sized by its width, its foot had landed within a pixel of the edge, corners and all, and the maintainer asked for it cropped).
- Settings' illustrations are drawn on the page's grey instead of Settings' blue-grey (#F0F4F9), so they sit on their tiles without a frame. The languages tile names each language in itself, in rows drifting by.
- The film is a 1080p copy of the master at 5 MB, lighter than the README's 720p one; it loads only when played. Pictures are WebP at their size and twice it, 7 to 150 KB each.
- Google's fonts load from Google, as the extension's do. Nothing else is fetched from anyone else, and there are no analytics.

**Changed (16 November 2026):**

- On a phone or a tablet, which can't add extensions, Add to Chrome becomes "Send to my computer": the device's share sheet with the store's link (AirDrop, Messages, Mail to yourself...), or the link copied where there's none, and the small print says it's for Chrome on your computer rather than which browsers. Touch without hover decides it, not the window's width, so a narrow window on a computer keeps Add to Chrome. Nothing goes through a server, and without the script the button stays a link to the store.
- The bar on a phone is a size smaller (52px, 8px inside, the name 15px, the button 14px, saying "Share link", since it opens the share sheet), as at 56px with a 15px "Add to Chrome" the name and the button nearly touched at 390px, and the button ran over the name and out of the bar at 320px. Below 360px only the icon stays. A wide screen's bar is unchanged.
- A design review (16 November 2026) tightened the craft across the pages: the little things' cards keep the tools' tiles' 40px margins, so their words start on one line; notes on What's new and Privacy run about 70 characters a line, not 90, their list dots hanging in the margin from a tablet up; numbers and version labels share their headings' baselines, the policy's numbers set as the steps' are; "Earlier versions" sits on its list's edge; code is in Google Sans Code, the family's monospace, with a tighter tint; dates read one way everywhere ("13 November 2026"); and headings are in sentence case, "Privacy policy" included.
- The footer is the words "You're in good company.", in two rows as wide as the page, their letters' own edges on its edges and the tails of g, p and y on its foot; the o's of "good" are his eyes, following the pointer or the link in focus and blinking now and then (a flattening slit, timed as on an animator's chart, sometimes double, and with a long swing of his gaze). The first time it's reached, each row rises through its own window and fades in, the second a beat after the first, and he blinks as it settles; with reduced motion it only fades. On a laptop the name and links sit above it and the small print in the room beside "You're in", 3px above that row's baseline (on it exactly, the small words looked to sink); on a phone and a tablet they stack above it. Slogans, quotes and the companion himself were tried first: he's already big in the last call just above.
- The page is off-white, Google's Grey 50 (#F8F9FA), not white: the white screens bleeding off the tools' tiles ran into a white page at the tiles' right and foot, the tiles' rounded corners with them. A hairline drawn around the tiles, and a rim of the tiles' grey, were tried; an off-white page needs no added line, and the tiles' grey still reads against it. Raised things (the selected tab, a key) stay white. The tiles' grey went a step darker with it, from #F1F3F4 to #EBEDF0, between Google's Grey 100 and 200, so they keep about the contrast they had on white; #E8EAED read heavy. Settings' illustrations take the stylesheet's grey (`var(--paper)`), so they can't drift from the tiles again.
- The live edits' screen sits 2.3% lower in its tile, so the tile's foot crops it between the slide's footer and the editor's own bottom strip, cutting no words.
- The bar's rim is lit (16 November 2026): one hairline, as before, but a light up and to the left catches it there and fades along it, its underside a shade darker and its shadow leaning right; over the black tile the glint shows most. A second, inner shine line made the edge read twice as thick.
- The bar's motion, reviewed (16 November 2026): in, the glass fades in quickly (240ms) as it floats down 10px over 500ms on a curve that starts gently, so the float is seen; the page's own arrival curve had spent most of the drop in the first 80ms, behind a half-faded bar. Then the name, the links and Add to Chrome fade in, in reading order, 50ms apart. No scale: 2% of a bar this wide was a 24px zoom, twice the drop. Out, quicker (200ms) and accelerating back up, everything on the glass going with it; before, the name and links vanished first and left a blank pill to fade. It leaves only once the hero's name is 24px back below its line, so a scroll resting on the line can't flicker it, and a page opened already past the hero has it in place, without the float.
- On What's new and the privacy policy, where the bar is there from the start, it rests flat on the page at the top, a plain header with nothing yet under it, and lifts into its glass, rim and shadow over the first 48px of scroll, tied to the scroll, as Material's top app bar and iOS's bars do: floating over nothing, its shadow had nothing to explain it. More space under it would have kept that.
- A review of the rest of the site's motion (16 November 2026): things rising in as they scroll into view rise 24px, not 32, on a curve that starts gently and settles, as the footer's words do (on the page's arrival curve they jumped 13px in 75ms, then crept the last few pixels for a second), and what arrives together comes 90ms apart, top to bottom and left to right, so two tiles side by side read as two. Buttons give at once when pressed (100ms) and spring back slower, the play pill too; iOS's Safari shows the press only on a page that listens for touches, so the page does. Fills and transforms on hover only where there's a pointer to hover with, so a tapped button or link doesn't stay lit on a phone. Every underlined link fades its underline in, as the footer's do. What's new's tabs have one raised white thumb that slides and stretches to the tab picked, as a segmented control's does, and the tab's page fades up 8px as it swaps in; without the script the tab is raised on its own, as before.
- More of him (16 November 2026), from a review that rated the site 7.5/10: its delight was all at the top, the steps and the end, and nothing moved on the first screen until a pointer did. The hero's companion now rises from behind the film as the page opens, on a spring (CSS linear() from a damped spring's response, 9% past and back), reads the headline and glances at Add to Chrome, then looks back out; with nobody pointing for 4 seconds (on a phone, between taps), both companions look about now and then, coming back to you in between, as the footer's eyes already did. The last call's rises from its edge a beat after it arrives and blinks once up. Pressed, the footer's eyes squeeze into happy arcs, ^ ^, as his do. Pointing at or tapping the lit "superpowers" sets off another burst of sparkles. Going from page to page, the bar stays put and turns into the new page's while the page under it fades (cross-document view transitions; other browsers just change page). companion-motion.js returns look() and blink() for a page to direct him; Slides' toolbar doesn't use them, so he moves there as before.
- On a phone, the bar's button says "Share link", not "Send link": it opens the share sheet.
- Each scroll-triggered motion checked for when it plays (16 November 2026), on a laptop and a phone, scrolling as a reader does: what rises in, at 83–88% of the window; the hero's companion, rising as the page opens on a laptop, where he's at 60–75%, but on a phone, where he's at the screen's foot or below, held down until the film's edge is 15% up the window (he rises at 80%); the last call's, which had risen as the card arrived, a screen below him, now once the card's foot is in view; the footer's words once the middle of "good company." is above the window's foot, not when a third of the phrase showed, with "good company." still partly below. "Superpowers" lights once the reader has scrolled to the end of the steps, its stroke finishing under the swell: waiting for the eased stroke to be whole, it lit a third of the way up the window at a steady 600px a second, and above it at a quick 1200; now 56–61%, and 41–45% at a flick. The footer's words were marked .waiting, the page's own class for rising in, which hid the whole phrase 24px low and jumped it up as they began to rise; they're .unrisen now, and offset in em, as Safari resolves a percentage translate against a box other than the one it reports.
- Timing tested as people scroll (16 November 2026), not by jumps or at a steady speed: a mouse wheel's eased 100px notches in bursts with pauses to read, a trackpad's momentum decaying with macOS's 350ms time constant, and a phone's flicks decaying with iOS's normal deceleration. Everything starts where it should (what rises in at 80–87% of the window, "superpowers" lighting at 42–55%, the footer's words at the page's end), but a companion's timed rise, most of a second, was carried off the top by a flick or a run of notches before he was up, and waiting for the page to slow didn't help: a flick never slowed with him in view. So where he isn't in view as the page opens (the last call's, and the hero's on a phone) he comes up with the scroll, as the steps fill, following the edge he peeks over from the window's foot to a fifth of the way up it on a spring (a little stiffer than the CSS one, so he keeps up), trailing a quick scroll and bouncing when it stops, and ducking back down if it goes back. He's fully up by 49% of the window with a wheel, 29% with a trackpad and 16% with a phone's flick, and seen rising throughout.
- On a phone the hero's companion opened part way up, a lump of fur with his eyes behind the film (16 November 2026): with the film's edge at the screen's foot, the scroll-following rise put him a fifth of the way through it. Now any of his edge on screen as the page opens and he rises all the way, there and then; only a page that opens with it below the screen brings him up with the scroll. A scroll that stops part way through his rise has him finish it, and he checks again once the page settles (the font arriving moves the film's edge up), so he never rests half up.
- The sparks around "superpowers" keep off other words (16 November 2026): over "a few" on the line before, one read as a smudge on the words rather than a glint off this one. Each still goes a golden ratio's step on around the word, and a place over another word's letters is passed over for the next; with none clear in a few tries there's no spark that time. So they gather below the word and past its end, and above it only where the line above leaves room.
- Going to a page where the bar rests flat, it's flat sooner (16 November 2026): the old bar now leaves with the old page, in 160ms, and the new one fades in over 180ms, rather than the two crossfading over the whole 350ms, when the floating glass was still there a quarter of a second in.
- Opened directly, What's new and the privacy policy showed the bar floating for a moment, then settling flat (16 November 2026): the rule meant to start it flat picked those pages as the ones without a .hero, but their headers are .hero too, so it never applied, and the script's flattening animated through the bar's 0.3s colour transition. Their bars are marked .lifts in the HTML now, flat from the first frame.
- A sweep for the same kind of slip as the bar opening floating, a first frame that isn't the settled one (16 November 2026). Safari ran 20 to 34 transitions on every page as it loaded, on phone and laptop: it can run site.js before style.css applies, and every transition then played from the browser's defaults as the styles arrived, links fading in from blue, buttons into ink, the home page's hidden bar fading out. Nothing transitions now until the page is styled (site.js marks html.styled two frames after the stylesheet is in), and a passive check finds none on load in either engine. A link to What's new's #next opened on Out now and jumped to Coming next half a second later, as browsers match :target only once the page has loaded; a line in its head now sets html[data-tab] from the address before anything's drawn, and site.js keeps it in step, the :target rules staying for no script. What has its own pressed or picked look no longer also gets iOS's grey tap box. Checked and fine: links to a part of a page land 48–109px clear of the bar; with reduced motion only fades and colours change.

# The companion in real 3D on the website

**Problem:** the landing page's companion, icon and screens are flat: SVG and still captures. A companion you can see from every side, lit by the mouse, his fur moving as he turns, could make the page feel alive in a way flat drawings can't, and make people remember him.

**Evidence of demand:** none yet. This is an experiment, on the branch `feat/site-3d`, to find out whether it's worth what it costs in weight and work. Keep it only if it clearly beats the flat page side by side.

**Approach:**
- Start with one asset, the hero's companion peeking over the screen, since he's the page's character. Only if it works, try the icon's tile and the finale's companion. (The finale's is done; the icon's is card 710, The icon's companions in 3D, or not.)
- Load it with a dynamic `import()` only once the hero is on screen, WebGL works and the visitor hasn't asked for reduced motion or data. The prototypes rendered with three.js; the home page doesn't (see On the home page).
- Build him in code first (a sphere of instanced fur strands or shell-textured fur, two eyes), the way `npm run brand` draws him from code today, so he's reproducible and diffable. Move to a Blender model (glTF, meshopt-compressed) only if code can't get the look.
- Keep the SVG companion as the fallback, shown until the 3D one is ready and for anyone it doesn't run for. Same placement, so nothing jumps when it swaps.
- Keep his behaviour: eyes follow the pointer, he blinks and squishes when clicked (companion-motion.js), and the light leans toward the mouse like the rims do.

**Prototype (26 November 2026, deleted since):** a page under `site/lab/`, served only by `npm run site:serve`, showed today's drawn companion next to two 3D versions, peeking over a screen as on the home page, or whole and turnable:
- **Drawn the same way** (`site/lab/fur.js`): every strand a camera-facing ribbon, tapered, lit as hair (Kajiya–Kay with Marschner's three lobes: a highlight toward the root, a broader one toward the tip, light through the tips from behind), with the bun shadowing strands that grow away from a light and roots darkened by the fur around them. Strands finer than a pixel stay a pixel wide and fade to their coverage through alpha to coverage, so the outline is fuzz, not flicker. Short pile (his face) gets no highlights of its own: its sheen is the skin's, or it reads as glitter.
- **A, groomed in code** (`site/lab/companion-code.js`): 40,000 strands in 1,300 tufts that come to a point, a tall crown, shorter fur underneath, short plush on his face combed away from his eyes, blending into the body over a soft border (`faceness` in `rig.js`). Spiky, closest to the drawing's outline. 146 KB gzipped, almost all three.js.
- **B, groomed in Blender** (`scripts/lab/companion-blender.py`): 2,700 guides placed by the same rules; Blender's Interpolate Hair Curves grows 40,000 strands between them over the bun (a density mask keeps them from under the eyes), then Clump, Noise and Frizz. Soft and plush, like a pompom. Exported as line edges (`use_mesh_edges`), compressed by gltfpack at 10-bit positions (looks the same as 12, a third smaller). 683 KB gzipped (173 KB code, 510 KB model). `build/lab/companion.blend` renders as black hair in Blender and grooms by hand.
- **Shared** (`site/lab/rig.js`): the bun's shape and eyes from the drawing (`site/lab/companion-shape.json`), the springs and blink timings of `companion-motion.js`, velvet lids, eyes darkened and warmed where they sink into the fur, a studio's lights (warm key that follows the mouse, cool fill, two rims), Neutral tone mapping (AgX greyed the blacks), and a soft shadow under him when he's whole.
- **Painted look** (the default; Studio lit is the other): in the spirit of hand-drawn animation, not a copy of any studio's. Painted, the fine hair is hidden (`userData.only` says which look shows a mesh) and the drawing's spikes are drawn as an animator would, along his outline only (`outline.js`): each frame finds his outline on screen from the shape both versions are built from (3,000 surface points projected, about 0.1 ms), and lays a zigzag of 100 spikes along it by the drawing's rules: each base meeting the next, each leaning its own way, mirrored, a long crown, blunt tips as the drawing's 6-unit stroke, lumps in the outline. The zigzag is turned with his head so the crown stays on top, and only the crown belongs to his body, as an animator keeps a character's landmark on model through a turn: it sticks out as far as a tuft rooted on it would in 3D, `(r + len)·cos(β) − r` for the crown's root nearest the outline, β in front of or behind it, so at rest it's the drawing's crown exactly and it shortens as he nods. Every other spike keeps its shape, long ones included. The turn is read from his eyes; the shadow stays with the light, as a lit shadow does. Tried and dropped: every spike wearing the fur of the part of his body on the outline (3,200 tufts), first stepped 8 times a second and then every frame, which made spikes grow and shrink in place as he turned: on a turning ball every point on the outline moves toward you or away, never along it, so the outline hardly changes, and real fur hides what does change in layers and shows the turn in the fur across its face, which flat ink doesn't have. Then the crown plus 30 long tufts on his body, each sticking out by its real 3D amount: they rose and set at random places on his edge, which read as spikes being deleted (checked: nothing hid them). Also tried and dropped, as restless: lines boiling 8 times a second; new drawings on twos only while he moved; tips trailing on springs as he turned and settling after. A zigzag that didn't turn at all made his turning look stiff. Petting (`PET` in `outline.js`): a spike within 0.2 of his radius of the pointer (measured to the spike, root to tip) bends toward where the pointer is going across it, as far as it's fast (all the way, 0.8 rad, at 2.5 of his radii a second), on a springy spring (ω 22, ζ 0.3) that springs back a little past straight when the pointer has gone, then snaps exactly straight, so he's still at rest. The pointer's speed is smoothed over 60 ms so a stroke holds the fur over a moment. Painted only (the fine hair of Studio lit doesn't bend), and off with reduced motion. Bending a full radian made the crown's spikes cross each other. Without the fine hair, the eyes had bulged past his outline as he turned (see "Looking at the pointer" for how they're kept inside it). A band under the bases, hidden by the body, fills between the zigzag and the skin. A first try planted 2,400 spikes over the bun in 3D, which overlapped at every depth along the outline and crossed each other: busy, not drawn. The shadow is designed, not lit (`BRUSH_SHADE` in `rig.js`): one crescent on the lower left, its edge wobbling like a brush, leaning a little away from the mouse; the skin takes it by the bun's plain ellipsoid normal (`round`), so the carved sockets don't put lumps in its edge, and the spikes take it from their direction on screen, so they match the skin beside them. Flat colour, two tones of ink (`LIT`, `SHADE`); No tone mapping. Painted, his eyes are drawn as the 2D companion's are: each part (`lids` and `wobble` in `painted`) shows only inside the opening its lids leave, the 2D `aperture` with its numbers (`OPENING` in GLSL, in shares of the eye along his face, not its tilt): curved lids, the upper landing on the lower's curve, the lower lagging 17 ms; pressed, a happy arc (`SMILE`), the pupils sinking under it and hidden past 0.6; a blink dips his body 1.2% and drops his face 6/285; his face rides a beat late on its own spring (`FACE`); stretched, his eyes widen 14%. Edges by alpha to coverage, and an opening thinner than a pixel covers only that much, so shut lids leave no seam. The whites have the drawing's wobble (`wobblePoints` in `scripts/build-brand.mjs`, its seeds 7 and 9 now in `companion-shape.json`), drawn 7.5% bigger and cut back to it. A darting pupil smears, up to 35% longer along its way (`SMEAR`). The shadow where he sits is one flat shape with a crisp edge (`GROUND`), spreading as he squashes; the soft blur read as a photograph. Studio lit keeps its lid that cuts straight across, a squint when pressed, and the soft shadow. Left out on purpose: paper grain or painted texture on him (cel characters are flat), lines boiling or motion on twos (tried, restless).
- **Looking at the pointer** (`LOOK` in `rig.js`): his whole body turns toward it, as if it hung 420 px in front of the page, 0.32 of the way side to side and 0.25 up and down (under 30° however far the pointer is), and his pupils look as the 2D companion's do (`PUPILS` and `GLANCE`, the numbers of `extension/companion-motion.js`): each eye at the pointer from where it is on the page, worked out along the eye's own axes as they lie on the page (its tilt and his turn), all the way to a rim of white once the pointer is 2.5 eye widths away, leaning 0.28 of their room toward his nose; 10% wider while the pointer is over him; a swing of 1.75 rad within 400 ms brings a blink, 7 times in 10, no more often than every 1.4 s. Before, both pupils went the same way from his middle, only 0.55 of the way, as his turn did most of the looking. Turning 0.7 and 0.45 of the way, and then 0.5 and 0.38, took his far eye to his edge when the pointer was in a corner, where turning and tipping add up; 0.38 and 0.3 kept it on his face but still felt a little much. He used to turn only about 25° and leave the looking to his pupils; tried and dropped: pupils still, the body doing all the looking. Turned toward the pointer, the far eye still shows, narrowed; an earlier version tucked eyes into his head as they turned, which left him one-eyed, or with a ragged sliver of eye where the carved socket's rim cut it. Painted, the eyes are now drawn 0.4 in front of the skin (`DRAWN_PULL`), so no skin cuts them, and only where his body is: the skin marks its pixels in the stencil and every part of an eye tests it, so his outline cuts them off cleanly, and only the front of each eye is drawn (`cap`), as the rest, inside his head, showed as a tail once pulled in front; the pull fades only as an eye turns right away (facing 0.12 to 0), so his body hides one behind him. Studio lit still tucks the eyes in at his edge (facing 0.3 to 0.02), where his hair covers it.
- **At rest** (`BREATH` in `rig.js`): a slow breath (3.6 s, 0.8% taller) so he doesn't look frozen; the 2D companion has no breath, so this is new to him. Off with reduced motion. Checked: with the mouse still, frames one breath apart are identical, and with reduced motion every frame is.
- **Eyes are clean ovals**, as drawn: their depth is pulled 0.06 toward the camera (`pullForward` in `rig.js`), so the short fur hugging the rim never nibbles the white, and the pull fades as an eye turns from the camera, so he still hides them as he turns. Seen almost from behind, a hairline of the eye's edge can still show at his outline.
- **Grey specks were the page showing through.** Alpha to coverage leaves partial alpha in the pixels and the browser composites even an `alpha: false` canvas by it, so after each frame the stage clears the alpha channel back to 1.
- **Cost:** each builds in about 0.3 s on the main thread (a real page would use a worker). Both drawn together take about 2 ms a frame on an M1 Max (450 fps with vsync off). About 670k triangles each: a real page would need fewer strands on weaker GPUs.
- **The canvas is opaque, in the page's colour.** Alpha to coverage also writes its alpha into the samples, so on a transparent canvas the fur's edges glowed white. On the real hero, the canvas has to sit on a flat background, or the fur needs another way to soften its edges.
- `site/lab/` and `scripts/lab/` were deleted once the home page's companion was done, with three.js and its credit in THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.txt: 3.4 MB that the page doesn't use, never committed. This card is the record of what was tried. `build/lab/companion.blend` (gitignored) is the maintainer's to keep or not.

**Decision (26 November 2026):** A, built in code, Painted. The maintainer: "do it all, we'll keep code".

**On the home page** (`site/companion-3d.js`, hooked into `site.js`):
- **No three.js.** Unminified, three.js is about 420 KB gzipped, and without a bundler nothing can be shaken out of it. Painted needs only flat colour, a stencil, alpha to coverage and some matrices, so the module draws with WebGL 2 directly: 20 KB gzipped, everything included (it isn't minified, as nothing on the site is). Fur, studio lights, tone mapping and the ground shadow (unseen behind the film) are left out.
- **The hero's and the last call's.** Both companions peeking over an edge come to life, each once it's on screen. The icon's stay drawn (card 710).
- **Loaded late, swapped invisibly.** `site.js` imports it once the page has loaded and a companion is on screen, unless reduced motion or Save-Data is asked for. The drawing stays in the page; the 3D one takes it out (`.peek.real`, `display: none`, which also stops its script: `companion-motion.js` skips frames for a drawing with no size) in the frame he's first drawn in. That first frame is the drawing: its outline, spikes, eyes and pupils, its flat ink, no breath yet, looking where the drawing was looking (`site.js` passes the last point), pointed at if the pointer's over him. A moment later (400 ms) his two tones and his shadow ease in over 1.2 s, and he turns to look from there. The host keeps the drawing's height, so he can still be pointed at. Lost WebGL context, reduced motion turned on, or too slow a machine (below), and the drawing comes back, looking where he was. No WebGL 2: the module returns nothing and the drawing stays.
- **Too slow a machine** (`SLOW`): once the page has settled (3 s), if most of his last 60 frames while moving came more than 50 ms apart (under 20 a second), he gives the drawing back. Checked by delaying every frame to 70 ms: the drawing came back and followed the pointer; at the normal pace he stayed.
- **Placed as the drawing, and at rest he is the drawing.** The canvas is centred on the bun's middle where the drawing has it, 3.6 bun radii across (1.278 of `--peek`), the camera as far off as in the lab.
  - His spikes are the drawing's own: `drawnFur` is `furPath` of `scripts/build-brand.mjs` step for step, 120 spikes from its seed, mirrored as it mirrors them, with its lumpy outline. His outline is measured once, unturned, and what it differs from the drawing's oval by is kept with each slice, so at rest the zigzag lies exactly on the drawing's outline, and as he turns it follows his. They're stroked as the drawing strokes its path, 6 units wide: every side pushed out, each valley filled to where its two sides' strokes meet (a mitre: that's what closes the narrow gaps between the crown's long spikes), each tip rounded. Pushing out only the tips left the crown's spikes thin lines.
  - His eyes and pupils are fitted to the drawing when the module loads (`EYES`): each white's hand-drawn edge, as the camera sees it, is placed and sized until it lies on the drawing's, and each pupil until it's where the drawing has it, as big. In perspective, eyes nearer the camera than his outline drew bigger and further apart, and narrower the way each faces off.
  - Measured side by side with the drawing at the hero's size, at 1× and 2× on a laptop and 3× on a phone, thresholded and compared region by region: whites and pupils within half a CSS pixel and 1% of their size, the outline's area within 0.2%. At rest, the difference that stays is his painted shadow. The canvas is drawn at up to 3× the CSS pixels, so it's as sharp as the drawing on a 3× phone.
  - Compared in WebKit too (Playwright's build of Safari's engine, 27.2): the same at takeover and at rest, and he looks, squeezes, is petted, and gives the drawing back when the context's lost, with no errors.
- **Transparent canvas.** Alpha to coverage writes an eye's coverage into the alpha channel too, which glowed on a transparent canvas; the eyes are drawn with alpha masked off (`colorMask`), and as they're drawn only where his body is (the stencil), its alpha of 1 stays under them. So the lab's opaque canvas isn't needed.
- **Drawn on demand.** Only while a spring, a blink or a petted spike moves; breathing alone, 12 times a second; never off screen or in a hidden tab. Measured: 12 to 15 frames a second at rest (a blink is drawn at 60), 60 while the mouse moves, none scrolled away. Resized, zoomed or moved to another screen, the canvas follows (its device-pixel box where supported, and the resolution media query, which also covers Safari); Chromium's emulation of a change of pixel density sends neither, so a real move between screens is still to try. A frame takes 1.2 ms of main thread on an M1 Max, 2.7 ms with the CPU slowed 4× (DevTools' throttling, as a stand-in for a mid-range laptop): the 3,000 outline points are projected without allocating.
- **Pressed, petted, touched.** A click or a tap squeezes his eyes into happy arcs; a mouse or a finger stroked across his spikes bends them. Over him, `touch-action: pan-y`: a sideways drag pets him instead of being taken by the page, an up-and-down one still scrolls. Enter and Space press him, as the drawing's script does, but on the website he's decorative (`aria-hidden`, not focusable, as the drawing is): a tab stop for a toy before the page's content, inside `aria-hidden`, would be wrong.
- **Largest Contentful Paint** is the film either way: 300–400 ms with the 3D companion and without it (reduced motion), five loads each.

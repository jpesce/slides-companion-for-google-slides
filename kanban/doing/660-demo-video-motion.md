# The demo video moves like one continuous film

**Problem:** the demo video is nine separate vignettes. Each scene fades out to an empty background, waits, and builds the next one from nothing, so the film feels like a slideshow of clips rather than one presentation, which is the story the extension tells. Within scenes, a few moments break the polish: a one-frame snap, grey washes, a cursor parked on the hero shot, a crop through words.

**Evidence of demand:** a motion review of the v21 render (2026-11-20), measured frame by frame (mean frame-to-frame change) and from the After Effects keyframes:

- Every scene change is the same reset: window and headline out (0.2 s, Material's emphasized accelerate), 0.12 s of empty background, label and headline in, 0.3 s wait, window in (92 % → 100 %). Seven times, so about 4 s of 48 show no product, and the rhythm is metronomic.
- Links, 9.68–10.25 s: after the click the window holds black for 0.42 s, then the whole page appears in one frame (`time<2.5?0:…` on Page and Article in "Links stage v4"). That's the largest single-frame change in the film, about four times any other.
- Exits over dark content (Spotlight's full-screen Earth at 7.2 s, the pen scene's dip) fade black into the light background and pass through a muddy grey.
- Text entrances start at 14× their average speed (opacity out-speed 3500 %/s for a 0.4 s fade; 560 px/s for a 20 px rise), so about 40 % of the fade and 8 px of the rise land in the first frame, then crawl. The label → headline stagger is 0.06 s, which reads as simultaneous.
- Motion blur is off in every comp, including the Wikipedia scroll, the zoom push and the window scale.
- Timer: the push-in crops the speaker-notes pane mid-word ("AUDIE…", "Fiftee… befor…") for 5 s; the wide shot before it lasts 0.4 s, too short to read; the click ripple (0.5 s) outlives the menu, which closes instantly, so it blooms over "Today's agenda"; then the pointer rests on "Where we are 5 min" for 3 s.
- Spotlight: the pointer sits on the full-screen Earth for the whole hold.
- Floating notes: the notes window arrives with the slide, so it never shows that it floats.
- Live edits: one static wide shot of two shrunken windows (the editor's toolbar unreadable); the payoff, 128 → 129 in the slideshow at 43.0 s, has no camera support.
- Music: 108 bpm, fitted so only the end card lands on a downbeat; every other cut falls between beats (4.74, 13.27, 24.87, 36.48, 49.16, 60.59, 69.66 beats in).
- What already works, and stays: the pointer's smootherstep arcs and press, the companion peeking up and blinking on the title and end cards, interface sounds on their frames, the chime on the live update, the product's own timings (Spotlight's 150 ms open).

**Approach:** one window that stays on screen from Spotlight to Live edits. Its contents change as a slide advance would, the headline changes in place (fade-through, no reset), and layouts that differ morph rather than cut: the slideshow window slides right and shrinks to make room for the editor in Live edits; it hands over to presenter view for Timer. Then the moment fixes: the link's page fades in with its stage (no black hold); dark frames leave by closing the picture first, or by scaling down as they fade; text eases from about 3× its average speed over 0.55 s, with a 0.1 s stagger; motion blur at 180° on the scroll, zoom and window moves; the Timer crop on the pane's divider, with a 1 s wide shot first; the ripple 0.3 s and gone with its target; the pointer glides off the content after its last click; the notes window rises in a beat after the slide; Live edits pushes in on the editor as you type, then moves to the slideshow as it updates. Scene lengths in whole beats (0.553 s), or sound.mjs changes chords on the cuts, since it builds the music from the edit.

**Done when:**

Checked on the render, not on previews: pixel rows through the fastest frames (the morph at 35.0 s, the pan at 38.6 s) ramp evenly, about 12 grey levels a pixel with no steps, and 64 samples changes nothing visible. The window's edge speeds up 1, 3, 7, 11, 16, 22, 28, 36, 41 px a frame, with no uneven frame. In a still, thin text smeared by a 180° shutter looks doubled, as it does from a camera; in motion it reads as one blur.
